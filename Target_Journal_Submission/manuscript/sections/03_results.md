### 3.2. Validity within cohorts

The coverage realised in a single split varied considerably in the smallest cohort (2.5th percentile across splits down to {{h_covp025_min_yasa_norm_reg_ffbs*100:.0f}} % in ST, where each test half had 22 nights). ### 3.3. Efficiency
*Adaptive scale at a fixed centre (pre-specified criterion).* Scaling the width by the posterior standard deviation lowered the mean interval score relative to a constant width around the same posterior-mean centre in {{h_nlower_yasa_SCALEmu}} of 24 combinations for YASA and {{h_nlower_lgbm_sc_s0_SCALEmu}} for LightGBM (mean ratio {{h_meanratio_yasa_SCALEmu:.2f}} and {{h_meanratio_lgbm_sc_s0_SCALEmu:.2f}}), significantly in {{h_nbetter_yasa_SCALEmu}} and {{h_nbetter_lgbm_sc_s0_SCALEmu}}, and was never significantly worse; with the calibrated plug-in as centre the corresponding counts were {{h_nlower_yasa_SCALEcal}} and {{h_nlower_lgbm_sc_s0_SCALEcal}} (significant: {{h_nbetter_yasa_SCALEcal}} and {{h_nbetter_lgbm_sc_s0_SCALEcal}}; Supplementary S3). The posterior standard deviation was a better scale than mean epoch entropy (proposed lower in {{h_nlower_yasa_norm_entropy_reg}} and {{h_nlower_lgbm_sc_s0_norm_entropy_reg}} of 24), whereas the pre-specified regulariser $\bar\sigma$ cost efficiency: without it the interval score was lower in {{24-h_nlower_yasa_norm_ffbs}} and {{24-h_nlower_lgbm_sc_s0_norm_ffbs}} of 24 combinations (mean ratio of the pre-specified to the unregularised score {{h_meanratio_yasa_norm_ffbs:.2f}} and {{h_meanratio_lgbm_sc_s0_norm_ffbs:.2f}}).

*Comparison with limits of agreement.* For TST the proposed intervals had a lower interval score than every form of limits of agreement in every cohort and for both stagers (Fig. 2; Table 2): interval-score ratios versus distribution-free limits were {{min(h_ratio_yasa_SC_TST_scp_signed, h_ratio_yasa_ST_TST_scp_signed, h_ratio_yasa_HMC_TST_scp_signed):.2f}}–{{max(h_ratio_yasa_SC_TST_scp_signed, h_ratio_yasa_ST_TST_scp_signed, h_ratio_yasa_HMC_TST_scp_signed):.2f}} (YASA) and {{min(h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_SC_TST_scp_signed, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_ST_TST_scp_signed, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_HMC_TST_scp_signed):.2f}}–{{max(h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_SC_TST_scp_signed, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_ST_TST_scp_signed, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_HMC_TST_scp_signed):.2f}} (LightGBM; all Holm-adjusted p < 0.05), and versus regression-based limits {{min(h_ratio_yasa_SC_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_yasa_ST_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_yasa_HMC_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_SC_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_ST_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_HMC_TST_ba_reg):.2f}}–{{max(h_ratio_yasa_SC_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_yasa_ST_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_yasa_HMC_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_SC_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_ST_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_HMC_TST_ba_reg):.2f}} (significant in {{h_nbetterp_yasa_TST_ba_reg+h_nbetterp_lgbm_sc_s0_TST_ba_reg}} of 6 stager–cohort combinations; versus Bland–Altman limits in {{h_nbetterp_yasa_TST_ba_loa+h_nbetterp_lgbm_sc_s0_TST_ba_loa}} of 6). For the other parameters there was no consistent advantage. Across all 24 combinations the proposed intervals had a lower interval score than distribution-free limits in {{h_nlower_yasa_scp_signed}} (YASA) and {{h_nlower_lgbm_sc_s0_scp_signed}} (LightGBM), significantly so for {{h_list_better_yasa_scp_signed}} (YASA); excluding TST, the mean ratio was {{h_meanratio_noTST_yasa_scp_signed:.2f}} and {{h_meanratio_noTST_lgbm_sc_s0_scp_signed:.2f}}. ### 3.6. Error analysis
Non-covered nights had lower plug-in accuracy (median {{nc_yasa_acc_plugin_med_missed:.2f}} versus {{nc_yasa_acc_plugin_med_cov:.2f}}; Supplementary S12). The WASO failure in SC had a specific mechanism: the 10-h SC window contains long wake after the final awakening (median {{waso_postwake_SC:.0f}} min, versus {{waso_postwake_HMC:.0f}} min in HMC), and one spurious sleep epoch late in a sampled hypnogram moves the final awakening and converts this wake into WASO. On average {{waso_fraclate_SC*100:.0f}} % of the samples of an SC night placed the final awakening more than 60 min after the reference ({{waso_fraclate_HMC*100:.0f}} % in HMC), and this fraction correlated with the posterior-mean WASO error (Spearman ρ = {{waso_rho_SC:.2f}}).

### 3.8. Sensitivity analyses, robustness, flagging and cost
The pre-specified criterion of a positive width–error correlation (95 % CI excluding zero) was met in {{e13_spearman_met}} of {{e13_spearman_n}} stager–cohort–parameter combinations ({{e13_spearman_met_TST}} of {{e13_spearman_n_TST}} for TST, {{e13_spearman_met_WASO}} of {{e13_spearman_n_WASO}} for WASO; Fig. 5a–c; Supplementary S10). Coverage changed by at most {{e13_M100_maxdiff_cov*100:.1f}} percentage points with M = 100 instead of 1000 samples, remained {{e13_cal25_norm_reg_ffbs_covmin*100:.1f}}–{{e13_cal25_norm_reg_ffbs_covmax*100:.1f}} % when only 25 % of nights were used for calibration, and varied by at most {{seed_max_cov_range*100:.1f}} percentage points across {{n_seeds}} stager seeds (Supplementary S10). Under signal corruption of test nights all methods lost coverage (Fig. 4; Supplementary S13), but adaptive intervals less so because their width responded to the corrupted signal: with movement artefacts in 25 % of epochs, TST coverage was {{rob_cov_art25_TST_norm_reg_ffbs*100:.0f}} % for the proposed intervals versus {{rob_cov_art25_TST_scp_signed*100:.0f}} % for distribution-free and {{rob_cov_art25_TST_ba_loa*100:.0f}} % for Bland–Altman limits; under strong Gaussian noise no method remained valid. Interval width identified nights with REM-latency errors > 30 min better than mean epoch entropy (AUROC {{auroc_yasa_SC_REML_proposed_width:.2f}} versus {{auroc_yasa_SC_REML_mean_entropy:.2f}} in SC), but for TST the label-free difference between raw and recalibrated plug-in was the better flag in HMC ({{auroc_yasa_HMC_TST_abs_raw_minus_calibrated:.2f}} versus {{auroc_yasa_HMC_TST_proposed_width:.2f}}; Fig. 5d; Supplementary S14). Sampling (M = 1000) and parameter computation took {{eff_ffbs_s_M1000+eff_params_s_M1000:.2f}} s per night on one CPU core, against {{eff_stager_s:.1f}} s for YASA itself (Supplementary S15).
