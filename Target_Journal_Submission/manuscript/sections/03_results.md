### 3.3. Efficiency
*Adaptive scale at a fixed centre (comparison added after the first results).* Scaling the width by the posterior standard deviation lowered the mean interval score relative to a constant width around the same posterior-mean centre in {{h_nlower_yasa_SCALEmu}} of 24 combinations for YASA and {{h_nlower_lgbm_sc_s0_SCALEmu}} for LightGBM (significantly in {{h_nbetter_yasa_SCALEmu}} and {{h_nbetter_lgbm_sc_s0_SCALEmu}}; never significantly worse; Supplementary S3). Under the protocol's joint criterion (lower interval score with Holm p < 0.05 and a positive width–error correlation) the proposed intervals beat the fixed-centre constant-width intervals in {{conj_yasa_SCALEmu}} and {{conj_lgbm_sc_s0_SCALEmu}} of 24 combinations and Bland–Altman limits in {{conj_yasa_ba_loa}} and {{conj_lgbm_sc_s0_ba_loa}} (width–error correlations from the pre-specified design, Supplementary S10). The posterior standard deviation was a better scale than mean epoch entropy (proposed lower in {{h_nlower_yasa_norm_entropy_reg}} and {{h_nlower_lgbm_sc_s0_norm_entropy_reg}} of 24), whereas the pre-specified regulariser $\bar\sigma$ cost efficiency (without it the interval score was lower in {{24-h_nlower_yasa_norm_ffbs}} and {{24-h_nlower_lgbm_sc_s0_norm_ffbs}} of 24).

*Comparison with limits of agreement.* For TST the proposed intervals had a lower interval score than every form of limits of agreement in every cohort and for both stagers (Fig. 2; Table 2): ratios versus distribution-free limits were {{min(h_ratio_yasa_SC_TST_scp_signed, h_ratio_yasa_ST_TST_scp_signed, h_ratio_yasa_HMC_TST_scp_signed):.2f}}–{{max(h_ratio_yasa_SC_TST_scp_signed, h_ratio_yasa_ST_TST_scp_signed, h_ratio_yasa_HMC_TST_scp_signed):.2f}} (YASA) and {{min(h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_SC_TST_scp_signed, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_ST_TST_scp_signed, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_HMC_TST_scp_signed):.2f}}–{{max(h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_SC_TST_scp_signed, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_ST_TST_scp_signed, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_HMC_TST_scp_signed):.2f}} (LightGBM; all Holm-adjusted p < 0.05), and versus regression-based limits {{min(h_ratio_yasa_SC_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_yasa_ST_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_yasa_HMC_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_SC_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_ST_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_HMC_TST_ba_reg):.2f}}–{{max(h_ratio_yasa_SC_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_yasa_ST_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_yasa_HMC_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_SC_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_ST_TST_ba_reg, h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_HMC_TST_ba_reg):.2f}}. For the other parameters there was no consistent advantage: the proposed intervals had a lower interval score than distribution-free limits in {{h_nlower_yasa_scp_signed}} (YASA) and {{h_nlower_lgbm_sc_s0_scp_signed}} (LightGBM) of 24 combinations (mean ratio excluding TST {{h_meanratio_noTST_yasa_scp_signed:.2f}} and {{h_meanratio_noTST_lgbm_sc_s0_scp_signed:.2f}}) and were significantly worse for WASO in SC (ratios {{h_ratio_yasa_SC_WASO_scp_signed:.2f}} and {{h_ratio_lgbm_sc_s0_SC_WASO_scp_signed:.2f}}; Section 3.6).

### 3.4. Point estimates

The posterior mean added little to the recalibrated plug-in for TST and was markedly biased for WASO in SC ({{h_pe_yasa_w_SC_WASO_ffbs_mean_bias:+.0f}} versus {{h_pe_yasa_w_SC_WASO_plug_in_calibrated_bias:+.0f}} min). LightGBM results differed in detail (Supplementary S4).

### 3.5. Transport and local deployment
Intervals calibrated on SC did not transfer reliably (Fig. 3a, d; Supplementary S7). On HMC, N3 % coverage fell to {{x_cov_yasa_HMC_N3pct_norm_reg_ffbs*100:.0f}} % for the proposed intervals and to {{x_cov_yasa_HMC_N3pct_ba_loa*100:.0f}} %, {{x_cov_yasa_HMC_N3pct_ba_reg*100:.0f}} % and {{x_cov_yasa_HMC_N3pct_scp_signed*100:.0f}} % for Bland–Altman, regression-based and distribution-free limits (YASA), because the stager's N3 bias reversed between cohorts ({{bias_yasa_SC_N3pct:+.1f}} percentage points in SC, {{bias_yasa_HMC_N3pct:+.1f}} in HMC); for LightGBM, TST coverage on HMC fell to {{x_cov_lgbm_sc_s0_HMC_TST_norm_reg_ffbs*100:.0f}} %. A frontal F4-M1 derivation did not repair N3 % coverage ({{e14_cov_F4M1_N3pct_norm_reg_ffbs*100:.0f}} % versus {{e14_cov_C4M1_N3pct_norm_reg_ffbs*100:.0f}} % with C4-M1; the bipolar Fpz-Cz of SC and ST could not be tested on HMC), and population, era, equipment, setting and scoring rules (R&K versus AASM) are confounded between the cohorts.

When all components were fitted on a few manually scored nights of the target cohort (Fig. 3b, c, e, f; Supplementary S6), ten nights gave mean coverage over parameters of {{h_loc_cov_yasa_HMC_10_norm_reg_ffbs*100:.1f}} % on HMC and {{h_loc_cov_yasa_ST_10_norm_reg_ffbs*100:.1f}} % on ST for the proposed intervals (YASA) (Bland–Altman and regression-based limits: Supplementary S6); distribution-free limits need at least 19 calibration nights at α = 0.10. A site's realised coverage fell below 80 % in {{h_loc_p80_yasa_HMC_10_norm_reg_ffbs*100:.0f}} % (HMC) and {{h_loc_p80_yasa_ST_10_norm_reg_ffbs*100:.0f}} % (ST) of repetitions with ten nights and in {{h_loc_p80_yasa_HMC_30_norm_reg_ffbs*100:.0f}} % and {{h_loc_p80_yasa_ST_30_norm_reg_ffbs*100:.0f}} % with 30 (median over parameters). In HMC these risks were close to finite-sample theory for exchangeable data ({{r_risk_yasa_HMC_10_theory*100:.0f}}, {{r_risk_yasa_HMC_20_theory*100:.0f}} and {{r_risk_yasa_HMC_30_theory*100:.0f}} % predicted for 10, 20 and 30 nights; Supplementary S17); in ST only {{r_risk_yasa_ST_30_ntest}}–{{r_risk_yasa_ST_10_ntest}} test nights remained, so theory already gives {{r_risk_yasa_ST_10_theory*100:.0f}}–{{r_risk_yasa_ST_30_theory*100:.0f}} %. With ten nights, HMC intervals were {{(h_loc_widthratio_yasa_HMC_10-1)*100:.0f}} % wider than with the full calibration half (Supplementary S6).

### 3.6. Error analysis
Non-covered nights had lower plug-in accuracy (median {{nc_yasa_acc_plugin_med_missed:.2f}} versus {{nc_yasa_acc_plugin_med_cov:.2f}}; pre-specified design; Supplementary S12). The WASO failure in SC had a specific mechanism: the 10-h window contains long wake after the final awakening (median {{waso_postwake_SC:.0f}} min, versus {{waso_postwake_HMC:.1f}} min in HMC), and one spurious sleep epoch late in a sampled hypnogram turns this wake into WASO ({{waso_fraclate_SC*100:.0f}} % of samples of an SC night placed the final awakening more than 60 min late, {{waso_fraclate_HMC*100:.0f}} % in HMC; Spearman ρ = {{waso_rho_SC:.2f}} with the posterior-mean WASO error).

### 3.7. Review workload and clinical decisions
At the threshold TST < 6 h (prevalence {{h_dec_yasa_TST_prev*100:.0f}} % pooled; {{r_npos_yasa_HMC_TST}} of {{r_nnights_yasa_HMC_TST}} HMC nights), the proposed intervals lay entirely on one side of the threshold for {{h_dec_yasa_TST_norm_reg_ffbs_frac*100:.0f}} % of test nights, and {{h_dec_yasa_TST_norm_reg_ffbs_acc*100:.1f}} % of these decisions agreed with the reference (Fig. 5e–h; YASA, pooled over cohorts; Supplementary S8). The recalibrated plug-in was as accurate on the decided nights ({{r_dec_yasa_pooled_TST_norm_reg_ffbs_cal_plugin_acc_decided*100:.1f}} %) but correct for only {{r_dec_yasa_pooled_TST_norm_reg_ffbs_cal_plugin_acc_undecided*100:.0f}} % of the undecided ones, whereas classifying every night by its raw plug-in TST was correct for {{h_dec_yasa_TST_plugacc*100:.1f}} % (recalibrated: {{r_dec_yasa_pooled_TST_norm_reg_ffbs_cal_plugin_acc_all*100:.1f}} %). Ranking nights by interval width also worked as a triage rule for continuous errors: after reviewing the {{20}} % widest nights, {{tri_yasa_pooled_width_20*100:.0f}} % of TST errors above 30 min remained (random review: {{tri_yasa_pooled_random_20*100:.0f}} %; Fig. 5a, b). ### 3.8. Sensitivity analyses, robustness and flagging
The pre-specified criterion of a positive width–error correlation (95 % CI excluding zero) was met in {{e13_spearman_met}} of {{e13_spearman_n}} stager–cohort–parameter combinations ({{e13_spearman_met_TST}} of {{e13_spearman_n_TST}} for TST, {{e13_spearman_met_WASO}} of {{e13_spearman_n_WASO}} for WASO; Supplementary S10). Coverage was insensitive to the number of posterior samples (at most {{e13_M100_maxdiff_cov*100:.1f}} percentage points with M = 100), to using only 25 % of nights for calibration ({{e13_cal25_norm_reg_ffbs_covmin*100:.1f}}–{{e13_cal25_norm_reg_ffbs_covmax*100:.1f}} %) and to the stager seed (at most {{seed_max_cov_range*100:.1f}} percentage points across {{n_seeds}} seeds; pre-specified design; Supplementary S10). Under signal corruption of test nights most methods lost coverage, the adaptive ones less so (movement artefacts in 25 % of epochs: TST coverage {{rob_cov_art25_TST_norm_reg_ffbs*100:.0f}} % for the proposed intervals versus {{rob_cov_art25_TST_scp_signed*100:.0f}} % for distribution-free and {{rob_cov_art25_TST_ba_loa*100:.0f}} % for Bland–Altman limits; under strong Gaussian noise no method remained valid on average; Fig. 4; Supplementary S13). Interval width flagged nights with large errors better than mean epoch entropy for REM latency in SC, whereas the raw-minus-recalibrated difference was the better flag for TST in HMC (Fig. 5d; Supplementary S14).
