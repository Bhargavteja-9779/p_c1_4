### Genuine strengths (brief)
The deviation log (S1) is among the most candid I have seen. It records that the calibrated plug-in "reproduced most of the TST bias reduction first attributed to the posterior mean" (S1 item 3), and that the primary analysis was switched after review (item 7). The manuscript reports failures (WASO, transport, the joint criterion) instead of hiding them. The end-to-end protocol fits every label-dependent component on the calibration half, which is the right design. My spot checks reproduced the shipped numbers.

### Strongest Counter-Argument
The paper's title and Abstract sell "conformal calibration of posterior hypnogram samples" as beating limits of agreement for TST (interval-score ratio 0.64–0.86). But every LoA variant in that headline is centred on the stager's *raw* plug-in. Only the proposed method gets the step that does most of the work for TST: recalibrating the epoch probabilities on local nights. That step alone cuts the TST error SD from 40.1 to 30.7 min in HMC and from 17.3 to 9.9 min in ST (my recomputation from the per-night file). Give LoA the same recalibrated centre (a constant-width conformal interval around the calibrated plug-in, which the authors computed as `scp_abs_cal`) and the advantage shrinks to ratios of 0.84 (HMC), 0.86 (SC) and **1.18 (ST, i.e. worse)** for YASA, and 0.78, 0.81 and 0.85 for LightGBM. In ST, the method as specified (posterior-mean centre) is also beaten by the authors' own variant that keeps the calibrated plug-in as centre (IS 56.9 vs 42.8). Decomposing the TST gain over distribution-free LoA, recalibration accounts for about 47 % in HMC (IS 192 → 168 → 141), about 2 % in SC, and all of it in ST.

What remains is a 10–20 % efficiency gain from a label-free per-night spread, in two of three cohorts. A mean-entropy scale gets within 3–9 % of that gain without any HMM. Against the protocol's own pre-specified success criterion, the method beat Bland–Altman in only 5 of 24 (YASA) and 2 of 24 (LightGBM) combinations. "Validity" means marginal coverage averaged over 50 splits in random subject halves of the same archive. That coverage is about 84–85 % on exactly the nights a clinician would worry about. It fails between cohorts. Locally it carries the textbook finite-sample risk of any split-conformal interval, a risk that is identical for the constant-width comparator. A sceptic would conclude that the practical message is "recalibrate your stager on 20 local nights and report bias-corrected conformal limits". The posterior hypnogram sampling is an optional refinement for TST, not the contribution the title advertises.

### Challenge-by-challenge detail (as requested by the coordinator)

**DA-1: Is TST a win of posterior sampling or of recalibration? (CRITICAL, [FIXABLE-IN-REPO])**
*Location:* Abstract L33/L35, Highlight L23, §3.3 L127, Table 2, §4 L151, §5 L166.
*Counter-argument:* Distribution-free, Bland–Altman and regression LoA are all built around the raw plug-in (L91 a–c). Recalibration removes bias and also shrinks the scatter of TST errors (HMC SD 40.1 → 30.7 min). Against `scp_abs_cal`, a symmetric constant interval around the calibrated plug-in, the TST ratios are given in the CRITICAL row. In ST, the gain over LoA (0.64) comes entirely from recalibration, and the proposed method is 18 % worse than the recalibrated constant interval and 31 % worse than the same FFBS scale around the calibrated plug-in.
*Where the manuscript already answers:* S1 items 2–3 state this exact concern. §3.3 reports scale-at-fixed-centre ratios (mean 0.92). Fig. 2 plots "Constant (calibrated centre)". §4 L159 says recalibration "by itself removed most of the TST bias". The authors know about the confound; the headline does not reflect it.
*To defuse:* Add recalibrated LoA (constant and distribution-free signed, around the calibrated plug-in) as columns in Table 2. Report the TST gain over them in the Abstract. Reword the title and claims so that the contribution is the per-night adaptive scale given local recalibration. State that in ST the posterior-mean centre is worse than the calibrated plug-in.

**DA-2: "Valid per-night" (MAJOR, [FIXABLE-IN-REPO])**
*Counter-argument and evidence:* see table. The coverage-by-width gradient (95.7 → 84.3 % in HMC) means the per-night width is under-dispersed. The intervals adapt in the right direction but not enough. A clinician reading "per-night 90 %" will take it conditionally.
*Already answered:* §2.5 L88 (empirical validity only) and Limitation (i).
*To defuse:* Use "marginal 90 % coverage within cohorts" in the Highlights, Abstract and Conclusion. Report coverage by width tercile. Consider making the exploratory Mondrian variant the recommended configuration for TST if it holds up end to end.

**DA-3: Forking paths (MAJOR, [FIXABLE-IN-REPO])**
*Evidence:* see table. The headline "advantage for TST" was identified after the results. The thresholds were "added after the first results" (L105). TST is the only parameter of eight with a consistent win, so a reader cannot tell whether it is a parameter-specific mechanism (the authors' explanation, L155, which is plausible and supported by 6/6 width–error correlations) or the best of many.
*Defence available to authors:* TST wins in 6/6 stager–cohort cells against LoA, and the Spearman criterion was met for TST in 6/6. That consistency argues against pure chance, but not against the recalibration confound (DA-1).
*To defuse:* Put one sentence in the Abstract on the outcome of the pre-specified hypothesis (joint criterion: 5/24 and 2/24 versus Bland–Altman). Label post hoc results as such in the Abstract. Report the number of tests in the family.

**DA-4: Simpler alternatives (MAJOR, [FIXABLE-IN-REPO])**
*Evidence:* see table. My check partly vindicates the authors: a label-free per-night spread does add information for TST beyond level-dependent bias. It does not vindicate the specific machinery: posterior-mean centre, FFBS rather than independent sampling, the regulariser.
*To defuse:* Conformalise the regression-based LoA (scale the residual by the fitted mean absolute residual, then take the conformal quantile) and compare. Recommend the best-performing simple configuration (calibrated plug-in plus posterior-SD or entropy scale) explicitly.

**DA-5: What does a clinician gain? (MAJOR)**

*To defuse:* Add the recalibrated constant comparator to §3.7 and Fig. 5e–h. Express the gain as "nights needing manual review per 100". If possible, propagate the TST interval to a clinical index (for example a REI/AHI category on a cohort with respiratory scoring) **[NEEDS-NEW-DATA]**.

**DA-6: Two feature-based stagers versus "any stager" (MAJOR)**: see table. *Already answered* in part by Limitation (iv), which concedes it. The fix is wording (coverage is stager-agnostic by construction; efficiency is shown only for YASA features) or a third stager.

**DA-7: Transport failure and usability (MAJOR)**
*Evidence:* see table. Cross-cohort coverage fails (N3 % 38 %; LightGBM TST on HMC 51 %, L135). *To defuse:* Report the analytic Beta(k, n+1−k) risk next to the simulated one and derive the night count from it. Separate calibration-conditional risk from test-set noise. Drop or qualify "even 30 left a risk of 15 %". State that no temporal validation exists.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. **Recalibration-only explanation for TST:** The TST gain over LoA is largely the gain from recalibrating probabilities on local nights, which can be paired with any constant-width interval (DA-1). This explanation is simpler and fits the ST data better.
2. **Conformalised heteroscedastic LoA or entropy-scaled intervals:** These give most of the adaptive benefit without HMM or FFBS machinery. The entropy scale is within 3–9 % for TST. My re-analysis finds that conformalised level-dependent LoA lose to the posterior SD in HMC and SC but tie in ST (DA-4).
3. **Finite-sample conformal theory instead of simulation:** The local-deployment risk and the night-count recommendation follow from Beta(k, n+1−k) without experiments (DA-7).
4. **Review-everything baseline:** If labs already review automatically scored studies, the relevant comparison is the review time saved, not interval-score ratios.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- Scoring technologists and laboratory managers, who would bear the cost of 20+ manually scored local nights per site and per stager or software update.
- Vendors and regulators of autoscoring software, who would ship a model whose error statement is invalid until it is recalibrated locally.
- Patients with atypical recordings (the hardest-quartile nights), for whom coverage is about 85 %.
- Paediatric and severe-OSA populations, which are absent from all three cohorts.

### Unexamined Premise
The paper assumes that a per-night interval on the *raw-scorer-referenced* value is what a clinician needs. In practice, the clinically consequential use of TST in PSG is as the denominator of respiratory and movement indices and as an input to insomnia and hypersomnia criteria. The paper never shows that a ±45-min TST interval changes any downstream classification compared with the recalibrated point estimate. This premise bears on the "so what" claim; it is carried as part of **DA-5 (MAJOR)**.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- The deviation log (S1) is a model of transparency and partly pre-empts DA-1 and DA-3. The problem is that the Abstract and Highlights do not inherit its caution.
- The WASO mechanism analysis (§3.6) is a genuinely useful negative result for anyone summarising sampled hypnograms.
- The robustness result (TST coverage 86 % vs 70 % and 51 % under 25 % artefacts) is a real advantage of adaptive widths over LoA and could carry more of the clinical argument than the clean-signal efficiency ratios.

## Recommendation
**Major Revision.** Confidence 3/5 (basis: re-analysis of shipped per-night data and tables; not a full re-run of the pipeline).

The paper's empirical work is sound and its disclosure is honest. Its headline attribution (posterior hypnogram sampling beats LoA for TST) is not supported as stated once LoA receive the same recalibration step. All CRITICAL and MAJOR items except a clinical endpoint, a third stager and temporal validation can be fixed from data already in the repository.

### The three challenges the authors most need to answer
1. **DA-1:** Show TST efficiency against recalibrated LoA (constant and distribution-free, around the calibrated plug-in) in Table 2 and the Abstract, and reframe the contribution accordingly. This includes the ST reversal.
2. **DA-2 and DA-3:** Replace "valid per-night" with "marginal coverage within cohort". Report coverage by width stratum. State the outcome of the pre-specified joint criterion in the Abstract and label post hoc results as such.
3. **DA-7 and DA-5:** Derive the local-night recommendation from finite-sample conformal theory, separating it from test-set noise. Show that the deployment risk is not method-specific. Quantify the clinical gain against a recalibrated constant interval, preferably on a downstream clinical quantity.
