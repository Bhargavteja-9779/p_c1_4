The study protocol (above) is held in the study repository together with its version history; it was not registered in an external registry. The repository history cannot date it independently: its first commit also contains the first interval outputs, so the statement that it preceded them rests on the authors' records. Changes to the analysis after the protocol was fixed are listed here in the order in which they were made, with the reason for each. Unless stated otherwise the proposed method itself was not changed.

**Before any interval result**

1. *Emission-temperature grid.* The grid for τ was extended from [0.10, 1.50] to [0.02, 1.50] because, in a smoke test on 12 nights per cohort, the fitted τ lay on the lower boundary. τ is fitted on labelled source nights only.

**After the first within-cohort results (pre-specified design, E04/E07)**

2. *Comparators that separate local calibration from posterior averaging.* The plug-in of the locally recalibrated probabilities and the plug-in of HMM-smoothed marginals were added as point estimates, and a constant-width conformal interval around the calibrated plug-in was added as a comparator. Reason: the posterior mean uses within-cohort calibration whereas the original plug-in uses raw stager output, so part of the TST improvement could be due to calibration alone.
3. *Bias-corrected comparators and a centre × scale factorial.* The calibrated plug-in reproduced most of the TST bias reduction first attributed to the posterior mean, so the original constant-width comparators (centred on the raw, biased plug-in) conflated calibration with adaptivity. We added bias-corrected constant-width split-conformal limits (a distribution-free analogue of Bland–Altman limits) and a factorial of centres (raw plug-in, calibrated plug-in, posterior mean) crossed with scales (constant, posterior SD). Claims about point estimates and efficiency were rewritten accordingly.
4. *Definition of σ̄.* The regulariser of the normalised score, the median of the posterior SD, was originally computed on the calibration nights only. It is now computed over calibration and test nights (it requires no labels), so that the score is a symmetric function of all nights' features and the split-conformal guarantee is exact when scores are not cross-fitted. The numerical difference was negligible; all conformal evaluations were re-run.
5. *Exploratory conditional coverage.* Coverage of the proposed intervals was 81–85 % in the quartile of nights with the lowest plug-in accuracy. Mondrian (group-conditional) calibration with two bins defined by label-free difficulty proxies (mean epoch entropy; posterior SD) was added as an exploratory analysis.
6. *Reporting.* For few-night recalibration we report the probability that a site's realised coverage falls below 0.80, not only mean coverage. The bootstrap returns no confidence interval when a statistic is undefined in more than half of the resamples; AUROCs based on fewer than 10 events are flagged as not interpretable.

**Primary analysis changed after an internal critical review of the first manuscript draft**

7. *End-to-end protocol as the primary within-cohort analysis (deviation from the protocol).* In the pre-specified design the HMM and recalibration components were cross-fitted over about 80 % of each cohort, including labels of subjects in the test half, whereas the comparators used only the 50 % calibration half (unequal use of labelled data). In the end-to-end protocol every label-dependent component (recalibration, prior, transitions, τ, conformal quantile) is fitted on the calibration half of 50 subject-grouped 50/50 splits only, with calibration scores cross-fitted inside the calibration half. The local-deployment analysis was reduced from 500 to 50 repetitions and n_t = 50 was dropped. The pre-specified analysis is retained in Supplementary S9 and leads to the same qualitative conclusions.
8. *Validity guarantee.* Because calibration scores are cross-fitted while test nights use the model fitted on all calibration nights, the design is the K-fold analogue of the jackknife of Barber et al. (2021), for which that paper gives no distribution-free guarantee (CV+ applies the fold models to test nights and guarantees coverage of at least 1 − 2α minus a small finite-sample term). Validity is therefore assessed empirically (coverage over 50 splits), not claimed as a finite-sample guarantee.
9. *Leakage fix.* Automatic hypnograms are evaluated on all epochs of the analysis window; the reference "unscored" mask is no longer applied to them because it is label information. Unscored reference epochs are treated as wake. All night summaries and downstream evaluations were re-run.
10. *Comparators added.* Regression-based limits of agreement (after Bland and Altman 1999, with the automatic value as regressor) and distribution-free limits from signed residuals (exact for exchangeable nights); parametric limits became a t-based prediction interval (previously z-based); the CQR features were extended (plug-in and calibrated plug-in values, posterior mean and SD, mean epoch entropy and maximum probability; linear quantile regression fitted on half of the calibration subjects, with a quantile-crossing guard); scale-only ablations at a fixed centre were added (earlier ablations changed centre and scale together).
11. *Statistics.* Paired comparisons changed from the Wilcoxon signed-rank test (a test of the pseudo-median) to a two-sided sign-flip permutation test of the mean per-subject difference, so that the reported ratio of means, its subject-bootstrap interval and the p-value come from the same analysis; Holm adjustment within stager × cohort; the Wilcoxon p-values are kept as a secondary column and leave-one-subject-out coverage intervals are not used for claims. The comparisons of scaled with constant-width intervals at fixed centres were added after the first results and form a separate Holm family. The AUROC bootstrap used 1000 instead of 2000 resamples.
12. *Success criteria.* The protocol's criterion for adaptivity was joint: the proposed method counts as better than constant-width intervals (Bland–Altman and split-conformal absolute-residual limits) only if its interval score is lower (Holm-adjusted p < 0.05) and its width–error Spearman correlation is positive with a confidence interval excluding zero. The manuscript reports the two conditions separately and jointly, including the fixed-centre comparators added after the first results.
13. *Items of the protocol run late.* The Spearman criterion, the number of posterior samples M and the calibration-set size (E13) were run after the first draft. The 50 Hz line-noise corruption was not run, because YASA's band-pass filtering removes it before feature extraction, so the test would not exercise the method.
14. *New sensitivity analyses.* Montage sensitivity (YASA with F4-M1 on HMC; source-only SC→HMC); clinical decision thresholds (TST < 360 min, SOL > 30 min, WASO > 30 min, REM latency ≤ 15 min; fixed before the analysis was run) and an easier/harder-night stratification; a label-free flag based on the difference between raw and recalibrated plug-in estimates; one-off computational costs and peak memory for a 7 × 24 h recording (measured from the stored predictions by a separate script; the per-night timings come from the original timing run); subgroups require at least 20 distinct nights; non-finite conformal scores are treated as +∞.
15. *Reporting and framing.* The title and the claims were narrowed: the advantage over limits of agreement is specific to TST; the recommendation changed from about ten to at least 20 locally scored nights.
16. *Analyses added after a second internal review (the primary analysis is unchanged).* A plain split-conformal sensitivity analysis (calibration subjects halved; all components fitted on one half, scores computed on the other; Supplementary S17), decision analyses against the recalibrated plug-in and constant-width intervals around it (Supplementary S8), a comparison of the site-level coverage risk with finite-sample theory (Supplementary S17), median TST half-widths, coverage by interval-width tercile and the number of positive nights per threshold and cohort.
17. *Framing.* The abstract and conclusions now report the protocol's joint adaptivity criterion rather than the fixed-centre scale comparison, state that much of the TST gain over limits of agreement reflects recalibration, qualify "valid" as marginal coverage relative to one scorer, and describe the decision thresholds as illustrative.
18. *Analyses added after a third internal review (the primary analysis is unchanged).* Review-workload and triage analyses (Supplementary S19) and an independent validation on the Dreem Open Datasets, DOD-H and DOD-O, with five scorers per night (Supplementary S18): YASA applied unchanged (C3-M2 and EOG1-EOG2), the primary protocol repeated on the consensus labels, intervals calibrated on the consensus or on one technologist and tested against every technologist, SC-calibrated intervals applied unchanged, and local calibration on DOD-O. The 56th DOD-O record, which has no consensus, was excluded as in the original cohort of 55 patients.
19. *Framing.* The title, abstract, introduction and discussion were reframed around the practical questions of a laboratory (calibration size, review triage, choice of reference scoring); Table 3 gives the resulting procedure, and the former Table 3 (point estimates) moved to Supplementary S4.
